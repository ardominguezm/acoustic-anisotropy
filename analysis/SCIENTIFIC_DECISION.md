# Scientific decision log

## Current paper status
Target: **Engineering Fracture Mechanics**

## Claims retained
- Bedding orientation changes the timing of AE-energy concentration relative to peak-load failure.
- In the analyzed dataset, 0°, 30°, and 45° reach maximum AE-energy rate in Stage IV, while 60° and 90° peak in Stage III.
- The earlier peak for 60°/90° is associated with lower tensile-event fractions and therefore a larger shear/mixed contribution.
- Acceleration strength is heterogeneous across orientations.

## Claims rejected
- A universal critical power-law for AE-energy acceleration.
- A confirmed trigonometrically parameterized angular law p_eff(beta).
- A Neural ODE / physics-guided deep-learning claim.
- A general predictive early-warning model.

## Confirmatory limitations
- One physical specimen per bedding angle.
- Exact angular-label permutation test for the proposed anisotropic law did not pass the confirmatory gate.
- Power law was never favored over an exponential alternative by ΔAICc > 2 in the tested sensitivity grid.
- Fracture-mode association is mechanistically suggestive but not externally validated.

## Manuscript framing
Mechanistic, stage-resolved discovery paper:
**bedding anisotropy shifts the timing and pathway of AE-energy release during shale fracture.**
